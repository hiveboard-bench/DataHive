Working with an AI assistant?

DataHive ships two skills that teach an AI coding assistant (such as Claude Code) how to use this system. Install them with datahive install-skill (this project) or datahive install-skill --user (all projects).

  • datahive-data-prep Get your data ready: the folder layout inside samples/, file formats, frequencies and sizes, the robot profile, annotations, and fixing datahive check / validate errors.
  • datahive-auto-collect Set up automatic collection: how the Runner and your robot script work together, and how to adapt the script to your own robot, cameras and controller.

Purpose

DataHive helps labs collect data for HiveBoard: run the trials, record and annotate each episode, check it against HiveBoard's rules, and upload it to your lab's dataset. Data stays in samples/ until you upload.

We collect

  • Robot state at 100 Hz or faster
  • Commands sent to the robot
  • RGB video from each profile camera
  • Trial outcome and annotation
  • Robot profile and board setup

Not collected yet

  • Depth images and point clouds
  • Odometry for actuated bases
  • Force, tactile, IMU or audio streams
  • Camera calibration

Questions

Runner or Annotate?

Runner plans, times and records the trials. Annotate is where you review, annotate, validate and upload episodes.

Manual or automatic mode?

Manual: you time the trial here and upload the HDF5 and one video per camera. Automatic: your robot script records the episode and you annotate it right after. Switch any time from the trial screen.

Can I manually add episodes from my own pipeline?

Yes. If you built your own collection pipeline instead of using the Runner's manual or automatic mode, you can copy your recorded episode files directly into the samples/ directory (under samples/{session_id}/episodes/). DataHive will automatically detect them so you can review, annotate, validate, and upload them in Annotate.

What must I upload in manual mode?

The HDF5 file and one video for every camera in your robot profile. The trial counts as valid only when all are uploaded and the episode passes validation.

Do I need a robot profile?

Yes. Fill in the required fields under Robot Profile first. Editing it later only affects new episodes.

What makes an episode valid?

A complete profile, 100 Hz or faster, 1 to 600 seconds, matching videos on every camera, and an annotation that agrees with the recording.

How many trials are needed?

Five on each of the 13 tasks, 65 in total. The Runner plans exactly that by default.

Where is my data?

On your machine, until you press Upload or Sync. Uploads go to the Hugging Face dataset set in your configuration.

Failure Causes Guide

Use this reference to distinguish between failure causes when annotating unsuccessful trials.

Cause Use when Discriminator
grasp_geometry The end-effector cannot engage the part in a stable grasp Grasp never acquired. Mismatch of jaw width, finger shape, or approach envelope
kinematic_limit The platform cannot produce the required motion Joint at range limit, singularity, or wrist roll exhausted mid-rotation
perception Alignment or state could not be judged reliably Confident but wrong alignment; occlusion at the decisive moment. Judgement failed
slip A grasp was acquired and then lost Relative motion between part and fingers after a stable hold
force_limit Available grasp or actuation force was insufficient Hold maintained, effort saturated, mechanism did not move
control_precision Control resolution too coarse for the task Target correct and reachable, but increment larger than the tolerance. Over/undershoot, binding, oscillation
other Nothing above fits Explain in Reason. Hardware faults, breakage, software failure
